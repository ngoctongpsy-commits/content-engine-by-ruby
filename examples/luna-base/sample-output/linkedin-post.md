August 2, 2026.

That is the date the EU AI Act becomes enforceable for high-risk AI systems. Most engineering leaders read it as a model vendor problem. It is not.

If your software ships into one of the eight Annex III categories (critical infrastructure, education, employment, credit scoring, law enforcement, and four others), the AI assistants your team uses to write that software land inside the Act's scope. Article 9 risk management, Article 13 transparency, and Article 14 human oversight all apply to the development workflow, not just to the deployed model.

The penalties are not theoretical. Article 99(4) sets the high-risk non-compliance ceiling at EUR 15 million or 3 percent of global turnover. Article 99(3) goes to EUR 35 million or 7 percent for prohibited use.

We see three gaps in most adoption plans:

1. No upstream specification artifact. AI-generated code cannot trace to authoritative intent.
2. Review at AI throughput. Reviewers default to syntactic checks when output exceeds human capacity.
3. Thin audit trails. A Git commit is not a conformity assessment.

The fix is methodology. Specification-Driven Development plus Test-Driven Development plus Human-Gated Development closes the gap. AI-in-the-Loop describes the architecture: AI is a participant in the methodology, not a substitute for it.

We unpacked the full Article-by-Article breakdown in this week's blog. Link in the first comment.
